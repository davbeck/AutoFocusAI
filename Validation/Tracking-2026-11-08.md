## Confirmed causes and targeted changes

Sparse endpoint observations missed or misrepresented the walk. Interpolation cannot recover an unobserved excursion; adaptive refinement runs only after sampled movement triggers a pan. The reference contains one subject throughout, so there is no evidence that subject selection caused this failure. This original November 8 case did not require a subject-selection change. Broader testing later exposed a selection defect with background people; see the [multiweek follow-up](Multiweek-2026-11-08.md).

Finer sampling also exposed a separate smoothness defect. The planner considered only the last step across the dead-zone boundary, discarding earlier walking inside the dead zone. A roughly 14px drift could request a roughly 485px recenter. The focused reproduction produced a pan beginning at 310.0s and ending at 309.75s, which jumped in one tracking step.

The planner now includes preceding observed movement in the same direction, bounded by the preceding completed pan. It stops at the onset of significant movement in the pan direction, so tiny stationary pose jitter or an orthogonal head dip does not begin an anticipatory pan. Independent review reproduced clipping with 1px/second jitter before a later walk; regression tests now verify containment with and without head movement. It keeps the existing dead zone, proportional linear pan, native crop geometry, and adaptive boundary refinement. No speed cap or end-time clamp was added.

## Validation and limitations

Regression coverage includes an out-and-back walk between the former five-second samples for both crop shapes, the measured slow boundary crossing, stationary jitter with and without head movement, existing fast/continuous pans, missing detections, interpolation, and subject continuity. Tests use Swift Testing, without sleeps or serialization.

Both crop dimensions remain 1920×1080 and 1080×1920. Export timing is corrected independently by the source-cadence PR; the baseline tracking branch still uses the previous timing implementation. No audio-drift inference is made from the old integer-rate metadata.

Only short representative source ranges were rendered. The entire 51-minute sermon has not been re-exported or visually approved. One-second sampling is a demonstrated improvement, not a guarantee for every speed, occlusion, or crowded stage. The existing vertical anchor/headroom policy is retained because it was comfortable in the checked interval.
